Before you start, a few things to know

In Scenario 1 the intensivist kept Margaret at Chadron instead of transferring her. Four days later she's ready to go home. This is what happens after the ICU, and how the program proves it worked.

  1. Open the prototype on Monitoring. If you've already played it, use Prototype › Reset prototype first.
  2. Click the Chadron Memorial tab: Margaret Hayes is there, on Med-Surg Bed 4. You don't need to play Scenario 1 first.
  3. The days pass in seconds. Her handoff and follow-up show the scenario's dates (her discharge is today); the lists show when each step happened on the real clock.
  4. It takes about a minute and a half to the outcomes, then as long as you want on the dashboard.
Some toasts come from the live feed. Other patients' alerts keep changing while you present. If a toast covers the one you want, click the stack to spread it.

1. Primary-care handoff

Day 4

At discharge the platform builds a one-page handoff to Margaret's clinic: what happened, the medication changes, daily-weight instructions and a 7-day follow-up. It goes to the clinic in a format it can read without a shared EHR. Nothing goes out on its own: the intensivist reviews it and sends it.

Steps

  1. Open Prototype at the bottom of the Sidebar and pick Scenario 6: After the ICU.
  2. About 2 seconds later a toast: Primary-care handoff ready. Click Review handoff.
  3. Her page opens on Discharge: Transition of care, Handoff to send. Read the page out: what happened, furosemide up to 60 mg, spironolactone held, the discharge weight and when to call.
  4. Scroll down: Send to clinic.

2. Readmission risk

Days 4 to 9

Her readmission risk is high: heart failure with a reduced ejection fraction, she lives alone, and she just had ICU-level care. Payer and rurality never enter that score; they're for reporting only. So the platform schedules a 72-hour nurse phone check and puts it on the hub tele-RN's list, and books her clinic visit within 7 days.

Steps

  1. Point at Readmission risk: High, its three factors, and "Payer and county rurality aren't inputs".
  2. Click Send to clinic. It's sent, and a moment later it says the clinic opened it. She goes home: her card leaves Monitoring.
  3. Under Follow-up, the 72-hour phone check (Ava Brooks, RN, hub tele-RN) and the clinic visit are Scheduled. Click Open Inbox.
  4. The Inbox: 72-hour nurse phone check, a Follow-up "For Ava Brooks, RN (hub tele-RN)", due in 72 h.
  5. About 16 seconds after you sent it, it's day 7: the call is done. Click Done: the row is there, by Ava Brooks. On day 9 her clinic visit is done too, 5 days after discharge.

3. The catch

Day 11

On day 11 Margaret comes to the Chadron ED with her weight up 1.5 kg and new orthopnea. The platform matches her to the earlier stay and alerts the hub. Her diuretic is adjusted in the ED and she goes home without admission.

Steps

  1. Click Monitoring in the Sidebar, then the Chadron Memorial tab.
  2. About 30 seconds after you sent the handoff, Margaret is back: ED · Bay 1, NEWS2 5, and a High alert, "Back 7 days after discharge, matched to her Chadron stay".
  3. Click her alert bar. Then Pathway on her NEWS2 line: Heart failure: diuretic adjustment in the ED (furosemide 80 mg IV, 80 mg by mouth from tomorrow, labs before she leaves, the criteria to go home). Click Accept, then close the alert.
  4. About 6 seconds later she goes home: her card leaves Monitoring and a toast says Readmission avoided.
  5. Optional: Inbox › the new Phone check the day after the ED visit › Open shows her Discharge section with Back in the network: the match, why she came, and Readmission avoided.

4. Outcomes dashboard

The pilot's month

This is the pilot dashboard, with one synthetic month that includes tonight's scenarios. Access: ICU-level coverage hours at the spoke sites, and the median time from alert to intensivist. Quality: transfers kept, bundle compliance, ICU-level length of stay, 30-day readmissions, mortality against benchmark. Affordability: transfer costs avoided and the revenue each hospital kept. Every number is against the baseline before the pilot. And it's synthetic: one made-up month for the demo.

Steps

  1. Click Open outcomes on the toast (or Analytics in the Sidebar).
  2. Say the month is synthetic (the page has no label for it), then point at the four headline tiles: 6 min to an intensivist, 61% kept at their hospital, 13.1% readmissions, $487K in transfer costs avoided.
  3. Scroll through Access, Quality and Affordability, then By hospital and Events this month includes: Margaret's two lines are there, with the other scenarios'.

5. Priority populations

One toggle stratifies every metric by payer and by county rurality, showing who benefited. The uninsured and the most rural counties started furthest behind and gained the most. And again: payer and rurality are used for reporting only, never in a score.

Steps

  1. Click By payer: every table shows Medicare, Dual-eligible, Medicaid, Commercial and Uninsured, each group's value and its change from its own baseline.
  2. Click By rurality: the same by county rurality code, RUCC 5 (Scotts Bluff) to RUCC 9 (Morrill).

6. Reporting

One click exports the quarterly DHHS report and the evidence pack for the next milestone payment. Measurement is built in, not a spreadsheet at the end of the quarter.

Steps

  1. Click Export quarterly report. A moment later, the Q4 2026 DHHS quarterly report and the Milestone 2 evidence pack, each with what it holds.
  2. Optional: click CSV on either to download it.
Close here. Only the measured cohort results from Intercept's existing service belong in the application as real outcomes; everything on this screen is synthetic.
