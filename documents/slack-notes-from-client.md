### IDEA 1: Enhancing Family Engagement Through a Tele-Critical Care Communication Platform

InterceptLive OS could bridge the communication gap between tele-critical care teams, patients’ families, and bedside clinicians through real-time notifications and seamless virtual engagement.

When a tele-critical care physician or advanced practice clinician (APC) initiates a patient encounter, the platform automatically sends a mobile notification to the patient’s designated family member or point of contact, informing them that their loved one is being evaluated.

Following the encounter, the platform offers two communication options:

- **Immediate Virtual Update:** The tele-critical care provider can invite the family member to a secure, HIPAA-compliant audiovisual session to discuss the patient's condition, care plan, and next steps. The bedside RN and other team members can also be invited through a mobile notification, facilitating coordinated, multidisciplinary communication.
- **Collaborative Scheduling:** An integrated, Calendly-like scheduling interface allows the tele-critical care team and family member to identify a mutually convenient time for a follow-up discussion, either virtually or at the bedside, with optional participation by the bedside RN.

The overarching goal is to transform family communication from a reactive, fragmented process into a proactive, coordinated, and accessible experience—improving transparency, family satisfaction, clinical collaboration, and potentially reducing the communication burden on clinical staff.

The platform would incorporate patient consent, authorized-contact verification, secure communications, and customizable notification preferences to protect privacy while maximizing engagement.

### IDEA 2: Intelligent Regional ICU Bed Management, Transfer, and Repatriation Platform

Real-time visibility into ICU and step-down bed availability across regional, tertiary, and quaternary hospitals, allowing rural hospitals, critical access hospitals, and independent freestanding emergency departments to make more informed decisions about patient transfers and resource utilization. Currently, rural hospitals have little visibility into regional ICU capacity until someone begins calling individual transfer centers, often across multiple competing hospital systems. This fragmented process delays care, consumes staff resources, and can result in unnecessary transfers.

Although bed management and transfer coordination platforms already exist (ie. Palantir), they are typically siloed within individual hospital systems; further, they are NOT CURRENTLY integrated with clinician assessment (although they are integrated with vital signs/labs/etc.) InterceptLive OS could provide a faster, more affordable, system-agnostic alternative—or simply interface with those existing platforms to create a unified view of regional critical care capacity.

#### Core Capabilities

1. **Real-Time Regional Bed Availability.** A centralized dashboard would display available ICU and step-down beds across participating hospitals, including staffing, specialty capabilities, and geographic proximity. Rural clinicians could quickly identify appropriate receiving facilities without making multiple phone calls.
2. **AI-Assisted Transfer Decisions.** AI would match patients to appropriate facilities based on clinical acuity, specialty needs, transport logistics, and bed availability. Predictive analytics could anticipate capacity constraints and identify the most appropriate destination before delays occur.
3. **Intelligent Patient Retention and EMTALA Protections.** For patients whose needs approach—but have not yet exceeded—a rural hospital's capabilities, InterceptLive OS could support continued local management for 24–48 hours, when clinically appropriate, with tele-critical care oversight while the patient's trajectory becomes clearer. Importantly, patients could remain on observation status (outpatient status), when medically appropriate, preserving the applicability of EMTALA during that period. Unlike a good-faith inpatient admission, observation status generally does not terminate EMTALA obligations. If the patient's needs subsequently exceed local capabilities, the rural hospital could initiate an EMTALA-compliant transfer, including invoking the receiving hospital's specialized-capability acceptance obligations when applicable. This approach could reduce unnecessary transfers, improve rural hospital revenue, and improve patient care without compromising timely escalation.
4. **Bidirectional Transfers and Repatriation.** The platform could function as a two-way bed marketplace. Once patients transferred to tertiary centers stabilize, InterceptLive OS could facilitate their return to the originating rural hospital for continued care. This would free higher-acuity beds, improve regional throughput, and return appropriate patient volume and revenue to rural facilities.
5. **Predictive Capacity Management and Interoperability.** AI could forecast regional bed availability, anticipate transfer demand, and identify bottlenecks. InterceptLive OS could operate independently or integrate with existing EHR, bed management, and transfer center systems, minimizing implementation costs and accelerating adoption.

### IDEA 3: Intercept Telehealth OS / ACCESS Connect

_Connecting Rural Acute Care to Longitudinal Population Health_

#### Overview

Intercept Telehealth OS could leverage its existing rural hospital presence to identify Medicare patients eligible for CMS's ACCESS Model and connect them with providers offering longitudinal chronic disease management.

By continuously monitoring hospitalized patients' diagnoses, laboratory results, vital signs, medications, and other clinical information, Intercept Telehealth OS could automatically flag patients potentially eligible for ACCESS during their hospitalization, creating opportunities for enrollment before discharge.

This would allow Intercept to bridge the gap between episodic acute care and ongoing population health management while creating new revenue opportunities for both Intercept and its rural hospital partners.

#### What Is the CMS ACCESS Model?

The Advancing Chronic Care with Effective, Scalable Solutions (ACCESS) Model is a 10-year CMS Innovation Center initiative launched in July 2026 that pays participating healthcare organizations to provide longitudinal management of chronic conditions, including through telehealth.

Unlike traditional fee-for-service Medicare reimbursement, ACCESS provides recurring, outcome-aligned payments based on patient management and achievement of measurable clinical outcomes.

Initial clinical tracks include:

- **Cardiometabolic:** Diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and cardiovascular disease.
- **Early cardiometabolic:** Hypertension, prediabetes, dyslipidemia, and qualifying obesity/overweight.
- **Behavioral health:** Depression and anxiety.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Chronic musculoskeletal pain.

Additional tracks, including substance use disorders, tobacco cessation, COPD, and heart failure, are planned for 2027.

ACCESS also provides a mechanism for eligible referring or co-managing clinicians to receive reimbursement for qualifying coordination activities with participating ACCESS providers.

For rural hospitals, ACCESS creates an opportunity to connect patients identified during acute-care encounters with reimbursed longitudinal services without necessarily building those services themselves.

#### Two Potential Business Models

##### Option 1: ACCESS Marketplace Only

Intercept develops ACCESS Connect, a technology-enabled marketplace connecting eligible rural hospital patients with independent ACCESS providers.

- Intercept Telehealth OS automatically identifies potentially eligible patients during hospitalization.
- Rural hospitals use the marketplace to connect patients with participating ACCESS providers.
- Patients select their preferred provider, who assumes responsibility for longitudinal care.
- Eligible hospital-affiliated clinicians or Rural Health Clinics can collect CMS co-management payments—currently $30 per qualifying service, plus a potential $10 initial onboarding add-on.
- Intercept generates recurring revenue by charging hospitals for marketplace access, eligibility screening, and care-transition technology.

**Advantage:** Creates a scalable, subscription-based business without requiring Intercept to provide longitudinal clinical care.

##### Option 2: Marketplace + Intercept ACCESS Care

Intercept operates the same marketplace but also establishes a separate Medicare-enrolled clinical entity to participate directly in ACCESS.

- Intercept Telehealth OS identifies eligible hospitalized patients.
- The marketplace allows patients to choose among independent ACCESS providers and Intercept's affiliated clinical entity.
- Intercept ACCESS Care provides longitudinal telehealth management of qualifying conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, and behavioral health disorders.
- The clinical entity receives CMS outcome-aligned payments, potentially generating additional recurring revenue.
- Rural hospital clinicians retain opportunities for qualifying co-management reimbursement.

**Advantage:** Intercept captures both hospital technology subscription revenue and CMS payments for longitudinal clinical care, although the latter requires additional clinical infrastructure and regulatory compliance.

#### Illustrative Revenue Opportunities

| Revenue Source                    | Potential Payment                                                   | Recipient                                       |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Marketplace subscription          | Negotiated monthly fee                                              | Intercept                                       |
| ACCESS co-management              | Up to approximately $100/patient/track/year for qualifying services | Eligible hospital-affiliated clinicians or RHCs |
| ACCESS cardiometabolic care       | Up to $420/patient in initial 12 months                             | Intercept ACCESS Care, if established           |
| ACCESS early cardiometabolic care | Up to $360/patient in initial 12 months                             | Intercept ACCESS Care, if established           |

ACCESS payments are subject to CMS eligibility, documentation, performance, and billing requirements. Co-management payments are not automatic referral or enrollment fees.

#### Strategic Opportunity

Intercept could begin with Option 1, requiring relatively little additional clinical infrastructure, while preserving the ability to expand into Option 2 once patient volume and financial viability are established.

Both models leverage the same underlying technology: Intercept Telehealth OS continuously identifies eligible patients, while ACCESS Connect facilitates their transition into longitudinal care.

Hospital subscriptions would need to reflect legitimate technology services rather than purchased referrals. An affiliated clinical entity would also require review of patient-choice safeguards, federal fraud-and-abuse/anti-kickback laws, and ACCESS-related billing restrictions.

The opportunity is to turn Intercept's future rural hospital footprint into a scalable population health platform—either as the marketplace connecting patients and providers or as both the marketplace and a direct provider of longitudinal ACCESS care.
